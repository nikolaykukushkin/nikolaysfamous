The rubric

Five things to judge, 30 points total. Don’t overthink the numbers — they’re mostly there to slow you down and make you taste. The ranking at the bottom is what actually decides the match.

Appearance 0–5

Colour, clarity, and the head. A clean pale gold with a white collar that lingers scores well; murky, flat, or a head that vanishes in seconds scores low.

0–1 lifeless · 2–3 fine, unremarkable · 4–5 bright, inviting, good retention

Aroma 0–5

Stick your nose in before you sip. Look for bready malt, floral or grassy hops, a clean lager character. Mark down skunk, wet cardboard, cooked corn, or nothing at all.

0–1 off or absent · 2–3 clean but quiet · 4–5 expressive and appealing

Flavour 0–10

The big one, double weight. Balance of malt sweetness and hop bitterness, depth, and the absence of off-flavours. Does it taste like something, or like cold fizzy water?

0–3 flawed or hollow · 4–6 solid, drinkable · 7–8 genuinely good · 9–10 you’d seek it out

Mouthfeel 0–5

Body and carbonation. Should feel crisp and lively, not thin and watery nor heavy and sticky. Lager wants a clean, brisk feel.

0–1 watery or flabby · 2–3 fine · 4–5 crisp, well-carbonated, satisfying

Finish & drinkability 0–5

What it leaves behind, and the honest question: would you pour a second? A clean, moreish finish scores high; a lingering metallic or harsh note scores low.

0–1 one was plenty · 2–3 happy enough · 4–5 immediately want another

How the match is decided. After scoring, force-rank the four cups 1–4 — no ties. That ranking is the decider: 1st = 4 points, 2nd = 3, 3rd = 2, 4th = 1, summed across everyone. Top two advance. Using ranks rather than raw averages is deliberate — it cancels out the fact that one person’s “7” is another’s “9”, so no single heavy or stingy scorer can swing the result. The 30-point total is only the tie-breaker, and good fuel for arguing afterwards.
